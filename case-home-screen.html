Case 02 · Home screen · Shipped

+34%more bookings per person,now that booking takes 2 steps, not 4.

Role
Sole product designer
Team
2 front-end + 1 back-end engineers, PM, CEO
Design time
3 weeks
01The problem

Meetup bookings were low.

The meetup is the product: the thing you turn up to and the thing you pay for. People followed clubs, but far too few ever booked one.

02The old way

Getting to one took four steps.

Pick a club→Join it→Browse its meetups→Book
03Why

Every step lost people, for a different reason.

01

You had to guess which club had it. “What can I do on Saturday?” only had an answer if you knew where to look.

02

Joining came before looking. We asked for commitment before showing anything worth committing to.

03

You only saw your own clubs. Every other meetup was invisible.

04The insight

They wanted a plan, not a club.

Six rounds of testing, regulars and newcomers alike: everyone went hunting for something to do.

05How I got there

Six rounds. The club page was never the problem.

01

Every tester went looking for a meetup, whatever we put in front of them.

02

We explained the club better. Nothing moved.

03

So I stopped fixing the club page. Meetup first, joining after.

06What I tried

My clubs in a row, everything else below.

The first two layouts put your own clubs’ meetups in a sideways row, with every other meetup in a vertical list. In testing people scrolled down, not across: they explored, and only the first meetup from their own clubs ever got seen.

07The design

The meetup is the home screen.

Every meetup on the platform, joined club or not, in one feed. The club became a name above its meetups, and joining moved to the end.

  1. Club name above each meetup
  2. One feed, scrolled the way people already scroll
  3. Filters for area, activity and day
  4. See it, then book it
08The impact
+34%

more meetups booked per person.

From 4.77 to 6.39 bookings a month. The people the club had been keeping out booked across more clubs, and more of them came back the month after.

09What I learned

Give the value first. Ask afterwards.

Joining a club was us asking first. Moving the ask to the end was the whole change.
